## Arrival and our first day

The flight leaves at 2:30 pm, so plenty of time for a lie-in before heading to the airport. At least that's what I thought on Thursday evening. I'm awake at 7:30, but after a hearty breakfast we still manage to get to the airport fairly late. Nele and Steffen, however, are even later.

Still at the gate in time, the bags are stowed and off we go. The plane has to take a small detour too, so we land in **Dubai** about an hour late. There isn't much transit time. After another 6 hours we land in Mauritius. The rest of the group even managed to sleep a little on the flight.

We get our backpacks quickly and the four of us squeeze into a battered Suzuki Swift and straight into left-hand traffic. Driving here turns out to be pretty relaxed, though. Little traffic and a roundabout every few kilometres. We drive along the coast towards **Le Morne**. We make a stop at the beach there, because the Airbnb won't be ready until around midday.

Completely knackered, we test the 25-degree water and afterwards most of us catch up on some sleep. Around 3 pm, after a few internet problems, we finally get into the apartment. The most important things, snacks and beer, had already been bought. So we enjoy our first evening on the balcony with a view of Le Morne.

After that we head out once more to find a restaurant and a few drinks, over which our eyes almost close. Then it's finally time for a well-earned 12 hours of sleep.

## Festival flop

We start the second day well rested. At the supermarket we stock up for the next few days. After a homemade breakfast on the balcony we don't set off for Le Morne beach until around 2 pm. Philipp and I test the new snorkelling gear, but unfortunately there aren't many living corals left.

In the evening we go to the **Divali Shopping Festival**, which unfortunately turns out to be a flop. After some street food we head back pretty quickly. But the evening is easily rescued with games and homemade mojitos on the balcony.

## Chamarel: waterfall and the Seven Coloured Earth

Today we start earlier. On the plan: **Chamarel Waterfall** and the **Seven Coloured Earth**.

We arrive around 9:30, ahead of most tourists, but in general we can't complain about the climate or the crowds. There's usually a pleasant breeze, especially in the mornings and evenings, and nowhere here is really overcrowded.

With a drop of around 100 m, Chamarel Waterfall is the highest on the island. The Seven Coloured Earth is even more impressive, though. Iron and aluminium oxides create the unusual colours and also keep them from mixing. Another highlight are the giant tortoises, which really do look ancient up close.

Fortified by a panini, we head to a spot a little further south in Le Morne. There you can watch all kinds of surfers, for example wing surfers, who hold the sail loosely in their hands. Looks pretty exhausting, we'd rather relax.

Today's snorkel scouting at least turns up two starfish and a huge sea urchin. But here, too, the underwater world isn't exactly colourful.

On the way back we're shocked to find the supermarket already closed. So no restocking the mojito supplies today, and we head for a few cocktails at a restaurant just 15 minutes away.

Of course we haven't learned from yesterday's mistake of not bringing a jacket, so we head home slightly shivering. After a short documentary about the dodo, it's bedtime for today too.

## No plan, all the way to the Tamarin Falls

Our plan is to drive to **Black River National Park**. Well, actually we set off without a plan and without a route. We're aiming for the **Tamarin Falls**.

With one bottle of water in our bags we meet a few sweaty Germans coming towards us in hiking boots, who tell us it takes at least 3 hours. So we pack a second bottle of water and start the Komoot route. We didn't think of snacks, because we thought it would be a quick walk.

With more than 500 metres of elevation, we too spend 3 hours climbing, but it's worth it! On a tough up and down we explore one waterfall pool after another, and in the last one we even dare to go for a swim.

The views are impressive and the hike, even though it was much more demanding than planned, absolutely worth it.

Slightly exhausted and hungry, we treat ourselves to a well-earned pizza, whose calories we'd easily burned beforehand. We spend the sunset on the beach at **Tamarin Bay**. That's where the river that flows through the waterfalls meets the sea. Afterwards we round off the evening on the balcony.

## Tamarin Bay

After yesterday's action-packed day we take it easy today. At 11 am, after a leisurely breakfast, we check out. Since the next Airbnb isn't available until 2 pm, we head back to Tamarin Bay, which is on the way. I'd hoped to rent a surfboard, but the waves are more like the Baltic Sea.

After check-in we watch the sunset. Since Philipp isn't feeling well today, only three of us try out the local pool bar. The bar gets our approval, but our skills still need work.

## Diving in Flic en Flac

Today is a bit more action-packed again. For Philipp and me the alarm goes off at 7:30, because we're going diving. We leave the car to the other two and trudge 20 minutes on foot to the dive shop, where I'd booked in advance via WhatsApp. With all our gear we board the boat to a dive site only about 10 minutes away called **"The Cathedral"**.

It gets its name from the steep rock blocks and caves that resemble a cathedral. We can dive into the largest one, and the light streaming in really does feel like a cathedral. The underwater world here is nowhere near as colourful as in Indonesia. There are hardly any colourful corals, mostly rock formations instead. But we can't complain: we spot a huge turtle, an octopus and a gigantic mangrove snapper in the cathedral.

The boat takes us back, where we warm up in the sun before heading out on the second trip around 11:30.

The next dive site is called **"Sweet Lips"** and is a bit more pleasant to dive, as there's hardly any current. Here we also see plenty of fish, moray eels and tiny creatures like miniature snails and shrimp. Plus a huge stonefish. Philipp apparently found it all a little too breathtaking, so he ran out of air during the safety stop. Luckily my air consumption was a bit better and I was able to help him quickly. Everyone made it back up safely.

The instructor just calmly commented: "Now you owe her a beer."

While we have something to eat and spend another hour on the beach, the other two are still busy hiking. Completely exhausted after about 10 km of up and down, Steffen and Nele get home around 6 pm.

So Philipp and I go looking for food on our own. Besides, Philipp still had to be shown the pool bar. I made up for yesterday's defeat with a good game. But after four games our eyes were closing too.

## Rum tasting in Chamarel

After about 10 hours of sleep, and the others even 12, we start the day relaxing on the beach. Today the sun is actually properly out for once. Most of the time it's cloudy, which honestly isn't bad at all, because that makes it really pleasant.

Today we head back to Chamarel, because we want to visit the **rum distillery** including a rum tasting. We're rather rushed through the tour, but since there are only French people here too, the English tour is almost a private tour.

After a short while we've already rushed through to the tasting, and there are no fewer than 13 rums and liqueurs to try. With the taste of the last rum still in our mouths, the next glass is already being poured. Don't worry, it's only ever a small sip, but we still take a taxi back afterwards.

On the way back we stop around 7 pm for a snack at **Big Willy's**, recommended to us by a taxi driver.

We actually hold out there for about 5 hours, but the big party we were promised never happens. The evening was still fun, and instead of music we make do with a few games.

Around 1 am we fall into bed completely shattered once again. Sun and sea air really do make you tired.

## Beach day

Afterwards we sizzle a bit more with the other two. With a little extra tan, Philipp and I head for the shower and later watch the sunset.

We pick up the others and fairly late we head for an Indian restaurant. Full of super delicious food, and of course with our freshly bought dodo card game, we stay a while longer. Some of us immediately have to take a bus ride with the dodo.

Then we pay the pool bar another visit. We take turns playing against two Indians and two Mauritians. ## Port Louis and a pool that isn't

After check-out we zip off in our Suzuki towards **Port Louis**. We're excited, because ahead of us is the last accommodation of the trip. Hopefully with a pool and balcony this time, so we can really enjoy the last few days.

After about 30 minutes we reach the capital and begin our sightseeing at a **Hindu temple**. Pretty garish, but somehow impressive at the same time. Then we want to wander through the city for a bit. But we quickly realise we've underestimated one small factor in our planning: it's Sunday. And the holy Sunday is evidently taken very seriously here. Many shops and sights close as early as 12 or 1 pm.

Although we'd read that **Chinatown** is said to be deserted on Sundays, we don't let that put us off and stop by anyway. Reality confirms the reviews pretty much exactly: the shutters are down, the streets empty and there's really not much going on. At least the **Central Market** is still open, even if Google once again didn't quite inform us correctly. Our market visit is rather short, though, as the musty smell isn't exactly inviting.

Our real destination is the food court and the Craft Market at the **Waterfront** anyway. Through an underpass we suddenly arrive in a completely different world. One moment we're among slightly run-down market stalls and empty streets, the next we're standing in a modern complex that reminds us a bit of Duisburg's Inner Harbour or Dortmund's harbour promenade. We stroll around a little, but find that the Craft Market is closed on Sundays and only reopens on Monday. The food court is also less spectacular than we'd hoped, but it's enough for a meal together. I do, however, find a pin for my backpack in a souvenir shop.

After a long search we finally arrive at the accommodation around 4 pm. At check-in I immediately ask where we'd find the pool. The answer makes our excitement vanish pretty quickly: the pool is currently closed and can't be used for our entire stay.

We paid around 600 euros for the accommodation. The pool was one of the main reasons for booking. Yet we weren't told a single word about this in advance. For us, that's simply outrageous.

So we don't just take it lying down. I call the host, who fortunately is understanding straight away and agrees to a full refund. Luckily I don't have to argue for long. Still, we now face a completely different problem: where to now? After all, it's already late Sunday afternoon, and finding comparable accommodation at short notice is anything but easy.

What follows is two to three hours of research, writing messages and comparing places. We send requests for several apartments via Airbnb and desperately try to find something suitable for the same day. But the replies are sobering: almost all of them are only available from tomorrow, even though Airbnb lists them as available from today.

With one host, though, we stay persistent. We message back and forth and keep trying to convince her until she finally offers to let us move in anyway. The catch: it hasn't been cleaned yet. So we make the beds ourselves and still pay for the night. For us, it's still the best compromise.

The apartment is only about two kilometres away and, at a whopping 200 euros more than our original accommodation, offers what feels like a whole apartment for us. And the best part: the pool belongs exclusively to our place and is all ours. True to the motto "you have to treat yourself sometimes", we go for it.

I also contact Airbnb and explain the whole situation. The original accommodation is fully refunded, and on top of that Airbnb gives us a small compensation for the inconvenience and all the lost time. At least a small bright spot after this rather nerve-racking afternoon.

We spend a really lovely evening at our very own pool, with a can of beer in hand and food delivered. That pretty much makes up for the extra cost already.

One small catch remains, though: the cleaning is scheduled for 8:30 the next morning …

## Botanical garden and a barbecue in November

At 7:30 we're woken by the cleaning crew. 8:30 had actually been agreed, so we start the day earlier than planned and all a bit groggy. After a mediocre breakfast but a very good coffee, we set off for the **Sir Seewoosagur Ramgoolam Botanical Garden** in Pamplemousses, the oldest botanical garden in the southern hemisphere.

Honestly, I don't find the garden that spectacular and have seen more beautiful botanical gardens. After Philipp himself is attacked by a goose, we have great fun watching more and more people get attacked at the very same spot. The geese certainly defended their island well.

After the botanical garden we head to the beach at **Péreybère**. Philipp and I have our daily snorkelling excursion, and this time it's actually a success! Along some rocks we see a few damselfish and even discover little striped eel catfish. The young ones hide in their hundreds in dense schools under the corals.

Unfortunately I didn't have the camera with me.

Since our new accommodation has a grill, the guys are looking forward to grilling a sea bream. Just having one more barbecue in November! About four hours later (the shopping and the grilling took a bit longer than expected), the guys enjoy their fish and we our grilled vegetables with potatoes.

By now we've also adopted two kittens, which we name Flic and Flac. Since Nele and Steffen miss their own cats, we probably won't be getting rid of the two any time soon.

By the way, mangoes grow above our pool, and I tried to harvest them with the pool net. Unfortunately without success so far.

## Catamaran tour

Sleeping in on holiday? Apparently not with us. Today we've booked a full-day **catamaran tour**. At 8:30 we're at the starting point and are loaded onto the catamaran together with some other tourists. We think we've secured good seats in the sun, but we thought wrong. In the front row, with plenty of swell, we get more than just a little wet.

Frozen and soaking wet, with only about 50% eyesight left thanks to sunscreen and salt water in our eyes, we arrive at **Flat Island**. First we refuel at a barbecue before moving on to **Îlot Gabriel**.

The snorkelling trip there is rather stressful, because I've never seen so many triggerfish. Triggerfish are extremely common here and very territorial. They defend their territory over 10 to 15 metres. Thank God you get a warning before an actual attack from the raised trigger. But even so, these fish have really big mouths that you really wouldn't want to be bitten by.

Luckily the trip back is much less splashy. At the apartment we finish off the leftovers and end the evening with a game.

## Wreck dive at 40 metres

6:50 am. Today the alarm goes off earlier than when I work from home, and for Philipp it's basically the middle of the night.

After a bit of trouble finding a dive spot, we actually found a dive school within walking distance. Most dive schools here only have one boat and around ten places per day. Once again among only French people, we become Philippe and Jeanine.

And luck is on our side today: first we head to one of the three wrecks off Mauritius. A **Japanese fishing boat** that was deliberately sunk in 1992. The hull actually lies at 40 metres and is the deepest dive so far for both of us. At this depth, the maximum no-decompression time is only about 5 to 10 minutes. The ambient pressure is 5 bar, so air is used up five times as fast.

Visibility is only about 15 metres and we descend into the blue until, after about 15 to 20 metres, the 57-metre ship suddenly appears in front of us. We circle the hull once and then swim over the ship to get back up to 30 metres.

Just the five of us, and this time even without the "grabby French", as Philipp and I call them. On the last dive, the French touched everything they spotted and held on everywhere. The number one rule, to touch absolutely nothing, apparently never got translated into French.

We see a huge stonefish, actually the most venomous fish in the world. Somewhat paradoxically, these fish are extremely poor swimmers. After just 22 minutes, the huge wreck slowly disappears beneath us again.

The second dive is our longest one yet. A full 60 minutes of marvelling at the most beautiful corals and fish. I always find the tiny creatures especially fascinating. Tiny little snails and a seamoth, though, you only spot if you look very closely.

For me, from about 40 minutes on, not freezing is usually a challenge. The dive school deserves a five-star rating for the warm shower afterwards alone. So I slowly thaw out under what feels like a 70-degree shower.

While we were diving, the rest of the group, by the way, went to see the dodo's bones at the museum in Port Louis.

At the pool we watch the cats manage to hunt after all. We also go for a short walk to the beach, and in the evening we pull ourselves together to go out once more. The pool bar we headed for is closed, though according to Google it's open as always. Google's opening hours and routes haven't let us down here for the first time. From a rooftop bar, though, I spot an empty pool table, so the evening is saved. After a rarely so bad game, I'm the first to leave the bar, dog-tired and looking forward to finally sleeping in.

## Pool day and the red church

Finally a day just the way Philipp likes it: sleeping in, starting the day slowly and first splashing around in the pool for three or four hours. We all needed that, though. At the pool we relax and soak up the last rays of sun. We also get word that we can check out around 5 pm the next day.

We do pull ourselves together for one last outing to a **church** just twelve minutes away. Right by the sea and with its red roof, it definitely looks very grand.

Afterwards we stop by the beach at Péreybère for an hour, and Philipp and I find the eel catfish nest again straight away. This time even with a camera! On the way back I spot the first ray of the trip. It turns out to be a small **electric ray**, which, like the electric eel, can produce electric shocks. Fascinating!

Already pretty hungry, we all head for the last restaurant of the evening. Stuffed and well fed, we decide not to make a late night of it today.

By the way, the mango was successfully harvested today too, only it was unfortunately rock hard.

## Last day and a long journey home

We start the last day relaxing by the pool too, as befits a departure day. Everyone is a little sad to leave the sun, but at the same time looking forward to home. No one is really motivated, though, when thinking about the roughly 22 hours still ahead of us.

After everyone has enjoyed the last warm rays of sun on their skin, we head to the beach once more to shop for the last souvenirs. The whole holiday I've been on the lookout for a crêpe with Nutella, and finally I find one. "Un crêpe avec Nutella, s'il vous plaît!" After years I can finally use my only French sentence and get an appreciative "Oh, you speak French!" in return. I have to add straight away that this really is my only sentence in French. I still get my crêpe.

We say goodbye to our two cats and head for a restaurant to enjoy the last sunset on the beach. A fantastic view and fantastic food make the evening an absolutely perfect finale to celebrate the holiday one more time.

Finally we set off for the airport at sunset and enjoy the panoramic drive and the setting sun bathing the Black River mountain range in beautiful light.

And then we're already on the plane to Düsseldorf. The three hours of transit in Dubai unfortunately turn into five, so after about 25 hours of travel we arrive home two hours late.

I'm looking forward to a little beer detox, cooking for myself and doing sport again. I'm absolutely not ready for sub-zero temperatures and mulled wine yet, though.

## My verdict

Mauritius is a super varied island where you can experience the perfect mix of hikes, excursions and beach days. The beaches aren't quite as paradisiacal as in Sri Lanka or the Keys, but there are still dreamy sunsets here.

The people are friendly, the towns not too loud and the island is generally pleasantly quiet. You can really wind down, switch off and relax.

I was especially thrilled by the **climate**. I've rarely had such pleasant weather on holiday: always warm, slightly cloudy and with a pleasant breeze. Just right, so you neither sweat nor freeze.

For me, Mauritius is definitely a destination that wins you over with its variety and its relaxed atmosphere!
