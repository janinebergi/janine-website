At 3:30 pm, the first flight took off. Seven hours to Dubai. A €4 phone mount
is all it takes to make Philipp happy and ready for 16 hours of One Piece back
to back. After a three-hour transit, it was another nine hours on to Bali.
Luckily, we both manage to sleep pretty well on the second flight. Maybe it's
because the Airbus A380 feels less like a plane and more like a flying hotel?

## Arrival in Uluwatu

With flattened backsides, we land safely in Bali. Immigration is
uncomplicated. We'd applied for all our entry documents online in advance.
Both backpacks show up right away, and it's another hour on to Uluwatu. With a delicious meal and an Aperol glass in a size exactly to my
taste (for just €6), we wind down the evening with live music.

The next morning I'm up bright and early at 9:00 am and head to the beach for
an early swim, while Philipp sleeps in, until at some point I get hungry.
Because a hungry Janine an hour later is not much fun. We fuel up for the
upcoming surf lesson with a tasty avocado toast and an influencer bowl for
Philipp.

Philipp takes it easy, while I wear myself out in the waves at **Padang
Padang Beach**, definitely the best waves I've ever surfed. The super-long
wave did have one downside, of course: endless paddling back. Workout done,
so the massage I'd booked for later was definitely earned. Afterward there
was freshly grilled corn on the cob on the beach, and no horrible sunburn
behind my knees or on my backside this time. We wound down the evening with a
tasty pizza at the same bar as the night before, even the waitress had
noticed we'd stayed a bit longer yesterday. The jet lag was clearly still
lingering.

### Beware of Monkeys

On day three, jet lag and the journey really catch up with us: we sleep until
noon. Around 2 pm, our first scooter ride on the island begins, for just 17
cents. Though I'd rather leave the driving to the locals.

We head to **Pura Luhur Temple**. This is where Philipp had his glasses
stolen four years ago. So this time his motto is: better to see nothing of
the temple than nothing for the rest of the trip. Everything gets packed away
monkey-proof. After witnessing a few monkey heists ourselves, including a
stolen handbag, passport and all, I hold my backpack a little tighter and we
stick close to a Balinese man armed with a stick. Thanks to the sheer number
of monkeys, we keep the visit short.

Just in time for the evening sun, we brave the climb down to a slightly less
touristy beach. The sweating was worth it. With a cold beer in hand, we
enjoy the beach, rocky as it is. For a little over €1, lazy tourists can of
course also get a ride back up. We let the evening wind down, pleasantly worn
out, at a local food place followed by a massage.

## Ubud: Cleansing Body and Mind

After a rainy morning and a slight scratchy throat, we change our plans:
Nusa Penida was already booked, but we push it back and head to **Ubud**
first. Three hours of traffic for just 50 km drags on. The roads are narrow
and the cars far too big. On the plus side, we get a lovely room here with an
outdoor shower and a pool view (in Germany, the same price gets you a
six-bed dorm in a youth hostel). After a burger in the cute town center, I
win a satisfying 4:1 at pool.

Woken up by around 20 crowing roosters, our driver Iko picks us up the next
morning. With Piet and Katie, a couple from Melbourne and Ireland, we first
head to the **rice terraces** (a bit too touristy for my taste, the fields
along the way were just as beautiful) and then to **Tirta Empul Temple**, to
cleanse body and mind. The cleansing is a Hindu ritual in which believers
bathe under sacred water springs to spiritually free themselves of negative
energy. Surely nothing can go wrong for the rest of the trip now, and I'm
certain the cold will be gone by tomorrow, too.

There was also a coffee and tea tasting, though coffee that a monkey had
already digested didn't really win me over. A ginger tea for the scratchy
throat did come along, though.

### Passive Volcano Climbing at Mount Batur

The alarm goes off at 2:50 am, even before the roosters. Off we go to
**Mount Batur**. I thought we had a volcano hike ahead of us, but no: along
with what feels like 200 other tourists, we're driven halfway up the mountain
in jeeps. Way too touristy for me. I much prefer small tours where you
actually get to experience the land and its people. The sun hides behind the
clouds, but we can see stars. Afterward, the hot springs pleasantly surprise
me, 36 to 45 degrees, at 8 in the morning. Luckily we could skip the next stop
on the itinerary, since we'd already seen the coffee plantation, otherwise we'd
never have made our booked 2 pm boat.

## Nusa Penida: Manta Rays and Private Reefs

All the luggage gets strapped to the roof, then the speedboat races toward
**Nusa Penida**. Honestly, that ride was one of my highlights, even though I
was hoping the backpacks wouldn't fall into the water. Our Airbnb, once
again, has a pool view.

Super nervous, I start the next day at 6:00 am. My last middle ear infection
has left its mark. Thank god Philipp is, as always, calm as ever. The
nearly hour-long boat ride with **Sanctum Dive** is not for the faint of heart: you get slammed
into the ceiling while some people relive their breakfast in reverse. I
actually enjoy it.

We pass some of the most beautiful coastline, including the famous
**Kelingking Beach**. On one of the most beautiful beaches in the world,
someone tried to build an elevator to bring down even more tourists. I don't
even want to capture an eyesore like that in a photo, and it makes me angry
that nature just isn't left as it is.

Dive gear on, and into the water: after about six minutes, the first
**manta ray** is spotted, simply breathtaking, any nervousness instantly
gone. Visibility is unfortunately a bit poor, but when the mantas appear in
front of you, it's simply breathtaking. **Crystal Bay**, the second spot, we
like a lot better: a steep coral reef and already noticeably less crowded
than Manta Point.

The next day we get lucky and basically end up with a private tour: just our
dive instructor Leo and the two of us. We have the reef, which drops steeply
along the coastline, entirely to ourselves, colorful corals and fish as far
as the eye can see. A light current lets us drift, completely relaxed, past
starfish and even four turtles. We didn't get to see a mola mola, the sunfish,
but the captain even invited us to come along to Tulamben; sadly the date
didn't fit our plans. Philipp turns out to be a fantastic underwater cameraman. That evening, of all people, Leo
plays guitar on a little stage. We can't say no to one more drink and a few
rounds of pool after that. Once again we're struck by how warm-hearted the
people here are, and we can wholeheartedly recommend **Sanctum Dive**. Thanks
again, too, for the tip to head on to Tulamben!

On our last morning before moving on, we stopped by **Kelingking Beach** once
more, but didn't go all the way down. This path, too, is guarded by monkeys,
and this time I'd rather have kept hold of my passport. The sight was sad all
the same: monkeys nibbling at plastic bottles amid the trash, and above it
all the giant construction crane that will probably stand there forever.

## Gili Trawangan: From Bicycle to Shipwreck

Turns out we booked more of a sauna boat than a speedboat to **Gili T**.
Sweating and stuck to our seats, we try to survive the ride, until, after
about 90 minutes, I finally realize there's an upper deck. We end the evening
with a pub quiz, where the biggest challenge is understanding the host
through her strong Irish accent.

Who's wide awake at 7:00 am despite having a later dive booked? Me. That
officially puts roosters at number one on my list of least favorite animals.
I grab a bike on the spot and circle the roughly eight-kilometer island in
just 40 minutes. Something I haven't mentioned yet: there are no cars and no
scooters on this island, only bicycles and little horse-drawn carriages,
exactly my kind of place. I also love how most tourists collectively decide
not to use the horse-drawn carriages as taxis, so they're used exclusively to
transport goods around the island.

The first dive doesn't quite blow me away, but I judged too soon: on the
second one we see several sharks sleeping in their caves under a coral reef,
and the highlight is a huge turtle sharing a cave with a shark. True to my
motto of "you only live once," I spontaneously decide to do the **Advanced
course**, which means cramming the theory and five dives into a day and a
half.

Next up: a deep dive, a wreck dive, and a night dive. Philipp joins for that
last one: in complete darkness, equipped with nothing but flashlights, we
head down to the **Bounty Wreck** off Gili Meno, the remains of a sunken pier.
Honestly, I felt like Tom Cruise or James Bond on a secret mission, even
though this dive had the strongest current yet and was anything but easy. The
next morning we head down once more to the Glenn Nusa wreck, which sank in
2016, and from here on out I'm an [**Advanced Open Water Diver**](/en/blog/advanced-open-water-diver-gili-t). I got really lucky with my dive instructor William from
England. We book the last ferry for tomorrow, because neither of us wants to
leave the island at all.

## Tulamben: One More Wreck to Finish

With only three days left, we shorten Canggu by a day: at **Tulamben**,
there's a shipwreck at 30 meters we haven't conquered yet, and that course
had to be worth it, after all. After about three to four hours by ferry and
another two hours by car, we arrive in a very poor region that lives entirely
off dive tourism. Four cockroaches greet us in our room, though at least
they're considerate enough to already be dead.

The first dive takes us down to the **USAT Liberty wreck**. The USAT Liberty
was torpedoed off Bali by a Japanese submarine in 1942 and deliberately
beached near Tulamben; when Mount Agung erupted in 1963, the wreck slid into
the sea and later became a famous dive site. The dive shop belongs to Sven, a
German expat who, whenever he speaks English, sounds like a Werner Herzog
impression, and they serve draft beer brewed to the German purity law. We
couldn't quite enjoy that first dive, though: water leaked into Philipp's
mask, my ear flat-out refused to equalize, and in the strong swell we were
both carrying a kilo too much lead, keeping our balance was no easy feat. The
second dive, the **Drop Off Wall**, lands in second place among the most
beautiful dive sites: a fascinating sheer wall that plunges 100 meters into
the water. As a grand finale, Philipp even got handed a tank with 270 bar.
You're usually happy with 210 or 220. After a four-hour drive for just 100
kilometers, at least in a comfortable car, dinner was a wonderfully tasty pizza
and bread with tzatziki. Clearly not enough bread, though: Philipp ended up just
spooning the tzatziki on its own.

## Canggu and Departure

Guess we didn't read the Airbnb listing carefully enough: construction site
right next door. But to put it in Philipp's words: makes you feel right at
home. We spend the day at a beach bar with a pool. There are worse things
than "having" to drink at least €35 worth of Aperol. After a massage and some
tasty noodles, we head to a pool hall, where two weeks of practice turns out
to have made zero difference.

On the last day, my trained surfer's eye isn't exactly impressed by the
waves, so instead we relax and work on our tan, after all, we spent half the
trip underwater. I hadn't seen this much Botox and silicone in a long time as on this beach.
Before the flight home there were still a few to-dos: pick up the laundry, one
last massage, and getting rid of our leftover small change at the market. Two
empty tubes of sunscreen and what feels like 20 Nasi
Gorengs later, we're sitting on the plane to Dubai. At least I win the very
last game of pool.

## My Verdict

I'm absolutely going to miss being able to get grilled corn on the cob or a
crêpe on the beach for €1.50, and of course, having someone cook delicious,
cheap food for us every day with live music. My absolute highlight was
**Nusa Penida and Gili Trawangan** with their amazing dives. Having a
30-degree-warm sea within biking distance is an absolute dream.

Everyone we met along the way was always incredibly helpful and courteous.
I'm not quite ready to trade that in for a bit of German grumpiness just yet.
But since Lombok and Komodo are still completely undiscovered, we'll probably
have to head back to Indonesia.
